A

Get them on file

Sales
01

Read the enquiry Email only

/inbox → /inbox/$threadId

The enquiry arrives as an email thread in the three-pane mail client. Triage it — status, assign, note, archive — then reply, or compose a fresh first contact. SLA tiles show inbound volume and first-response rate against target.

Skip if

They came in any other way — only email lands in the inbox, so there is no thread to read. Go to step 2, or straight to step 5 for a repeat booking. An emailed enquiry that never converts leaves a thread but no outcome — a thread can only be closed, never marked won or lost — which is why win rate cannot be measured outside the quotation ledger.

──
Chain break · Inbox → Customer

An inbound email can be triaged, assigned and replied to, but never turned into a customer. The sender's name, address and domain get retyped by hand in step 2. The database already reserves customer as a link target; the router refuses it.

02

Create the customer

/companies → Add

Search the directory first. Then press Add: Company title is the only mandatory field, plus at least one role — Customer, Supplier or Agent. Saving takes you straight to the edit form’s seven sections, five of them new since the dialog: classification, address, settlement terms (days payable, credit limit), the internal staff slots and the record.

Watch

Nothing forces this step — a quotation needs only a typed client name, so a walk-in can be quoted without ever existing as a company. Skipping it drops the buyer’s address, tax ID and payment terms from the invoice — those rows vanish, the name still prints. Duplicate protection is case-sensitive and there is no typeahead, so “Sunrise Sdn Bhd” and “SUNRISE SDN BHD” both save.

03

Record the person who called

/companies/$companyId → Contacts card

Add the counterparty's human: name, role, phone, email, primary flag. This is the only real table for counterparty people — the seven “personnel” fields on the company record are NCT's staff, not theirs.

B

Quote them

Sales · Sales admin · Reviewer
04

Publish the rate card

/fee-templates → /approve/template

Build the pivoted tariff: billing-unit columns (20GP, 40HQ, 40RF), fee rows carrying destination, minimum charge and if-any flags, an amount in at least one column on every selling row — a single cell may be blank (unpriced) or N/A (not available at that unit). Bind it to a company to make it that customer's deal. Only an approved selling template counts as a tariff.

Watch

The gate meant to stop an un-approved template being applied ships switched off, so nothing on the server refuses a draft card — only the picker’s default filter hides it, one tick away. Revising a card is Duplicate → edit → approve, then supersede the old one.

05

Raise the quotation

/quotations → /quotations/$quotationId

Fill the header: entrust unit, business type, incoterms, POL and POD, carrier, schedule, validity. Two remark fields — the annotation prints on the customer's sheet, the internal remark never does. Saving allocates NCT-Q-YYYYMM-#### and forces status to draft.

06

Price it

/quotations/$quotationId → fee grid

Record containers and batches. Then either tick tariff cells in the Reference-template dialog and apply them, or type fee lines by hand with side and segment. The FX table seeds each foreign line's rate; the footer computes the tax-inclusive total and estimated profit; each line gets a badge reading its price against the tariff.

Watch

Applied lines always land at quantity 1. The template never reads your container mix, so on a 3×40HQ booking every applied line is a third of the real charge until you retype the quantity. Nothing checks the cost side at all, so the margin rests on unverified buy figures.

07

Save against the tariff floor

/quotations/$quotationId → Save Quote

The server refuses the whole quotation if any selling line sits below its resolved tariff floor, and names every offending line. A price may be higher than tariff — never lower.

Watch

Two approved sheets with the same start date that disagree on a price leave a line “ambiguous”, which carries no floor — the refusal silently skips it. Two spellings of one charge unit do the same.

08

Get it approved

/approve/quotation

Submit for review. The queue is the quotation ledger in review mode, routed by open audit stage rather than by status. A reviewer approves or rejects with a reason; approval freezes the record.

09

Send it to the customer

/quotations/$quotationId → Send

Pick your mailbox, the recipient (pre-filled from the company’s primary contact; Cc is always typed), format and language. The server re-renders the sheet, mails it as an attachment, opens a thread, links that thread to the quotation and stamps the send. The export is an allow-list: every cost line and the margin are stripped.

Watch

There is no outbox. If the stamp fails after the mail goes out, the only way forward is pressing Send again — which mails the customer a second copy.

10

Record their answer

/quotations/$quotationId → Decide

Read the reply in the Threads card, then mark Won or Lost with the date they actually answered, their PO reference and a note. The server refuses if the quotation was never sent, and freezes the record afterwards.

Watch

A mis-keyed decision cannot be corrected. The code says so itself: fixing one currently needs a database edit. The customer never touches the system — there is no accept link and no signature capture.

C

Open the job

Sales · Operations · Reviewer
11

Convert the won quote into a job

/quotations/$quotationId → Convert to order

One press mints the order carrying the header, the company link, the containers and the receivable lines only — cost-side and if-any lines are deliberately left behind — then drops you on the order's edit form. It is one-shot: a quotation that already has an order is refused.

──
Chain break · Quotation → Order

The job arrives with no job number. Conversion never allocates one, so the order shows an em-dash where its identity belongs and every cost line it creates inherits the quotation number instead.

The repair is one press away: Assign number sits under the Job Number box on the edit form you land on, and on the record page. It is still a second step conversion should not require — hence step 12.

12

Stamp the job number by hand

/order/<trade>/$orderId → Assign number

Press Assign number — it sits under the Job Number box on the edit form, and on the record page — to allocate the next JOByymm##### serial. The button only renders while the number is blank.

Watch

Miss it and the job stays numberless, with any cost line added afterwards carrying a null order number — the lines conversion made keep the quotation number, so one invoice can print both. This is the single easiest step in the chain to skip and the most expensive to notice late.

13

Receive it into operations

/order/to-receive

The order lands unaccepted in the intake queue. Receive it, which stamps the acceptance time, or reject it with a reason. Re-receiving an accepted order is refused so the real receiving time survives.

──
Chain break · Intake queue → the job

The queue has no exit. You can accept or refuse a job here but you cannot open it — there is not one link out of the entire screen. Deciding means memorising the order number, walking to the right trade ledger and finding it again.

14

Fill in the operational detail

/order/$orderId/edit

One scrolling form with a section rail: business info, shipping space (vessel, voyage, ETD, ETA, ETB, cut-off), lading bills (shipper, consignee, notify, marks, weights), the desk roles, then containers, cargo lines, expense rows and appendages. Save sends only what changed and returns you to the trade ledger.

Watch

No field on any trade is mandatory. A sea-import order saves with no client, no vessel, no ports and no cargo. The desk roles are free-typed names, not linked members — so the only way to ask “which orders are mine” is a substring search on a typed name — no member link, no my-orders view, and a namesake changes the answer.

15

Get the order approved

/approve/order

Submit from the trade ledger’s row menu; this queue only decides — one row, or a whole selection from its toolbar. One review queue spans every trade, not just sea export.

D

Run it

Documentation · Operations
──
Chain break · Order → Bill of lading

There is no route from the job to its B/L. The bill is typed fresh and has to re-find its order in a flat 200-row dropdown, or match on a job number typed as free text — the exact string match that once picked the wrong row.

16

Raise the bill of lading

/lading/create

Type the B/L, or drop the carrier's PDF and let OCR pre-fill it. Point it at the job, then assign the bill's seven desk roles — unlike the order's, these are real linked members.

17

Move the B/L through its states

/lading → /lading/$id

Check In (endorsement), Amend, Check Out (release) and Hold (detention), each with a cancel twin and each behind its own gate. Per row, or from the detail page — the list’s tick-boxes drive review only. Scanned paperwork files as attachments on the bill itself.

Watch

The review half is missing: no shipped screen can submit a lading for review, so the Shipment review queue can only ever decide submissions that cannot be created.

18

Approve the carrier's MBL/HBL

/documents → /documents/$documentId

Upload the PDF; the pipeline classifies, extracts and validates it, then either auto-approves or parks it for review. Check each field against the scan and approve. That approval is what starts automated customs and port tracking — nothing else does.

Watch

There is no K1 or K2 customs-declaration type in the vocabulary, so the Sepangar filing itself lives outside this system.

──
Chain break · Documents → the job

Paperwork cannot be filed against the order it belongs to. The server accepts orders and ladings as link targets; the only screen that links anything sends the literal type quote. So the per-order Documents card queries a link nothing ever writes — and never renders at all, because the server sends documentCount and blStage while the client demands counts and customs.

19

Watch the demurrage clock

/customs-tracking → /overview

Two schedulers poll MYCIEDS and SBCP — customs three times a day, the port hourly — issuing three kinds of check. Customs clearance, vessel berthing and container gate-out arrive on their own, and the 72-hour countdown starts itself at berth. The board sorts by urgency and emails the owning branch on threshold and structural failures. A job the automation can never finish is voided with a written reason.

Watch

Tracking jobs are anchored to the document only — no order, no lading — and the board cannot reach that job's fees. The server does match jobs to orders by B/L number, but leaves the deadline out of that payload, and the card meant to show it never renders — so the countdown is still invisible from the order it belongs to.

E

Bill it

Operations · Accounting · Cashier
20

Enter what the job cost and what it bills

/order/$orderId/expenses

Two stacked grids, receivable above payable, saved together, with a live gross-profit box underneath showing both tax-inclusive and tax-exclusive. Copy to payable mirrors every named row across, minus the settlement party.

──
Chain break · The customer's identity ends here

This is the most consequential break in the chain. The order knows its customer's company record. The fee line it creates does not — the settlement party is written as a bare string, and the company reference is not even an accepted field on this path.

The bill inherits that null, and the invoice at step 25 resolves its buyer from it. Result: the document your customer receives has no address, no tax ID and no due date. Someone retypes them outside the system, or it goes out incomplete.

21

Get the fees reviewed

/approve/cost

Submit from the fee ledger’s Review menu; decide here, on a toolbar of Approved / Rejected / Refresh Page. The fee ledger’s own toolbar — not this one — carries reconcile, lock, archive, tag, and an exchange-rate dialog that applies a rate you type by hand.

──
Chain break · Order → Bill

You cannot bill a job from the job. The order's expense page has no Create bill, and its one link to the fee ledger carries no filter — even though that ledger accepts an order filter. You land on the org-wide list and re-find your own shipment by typing its number.

22

Group the fees into a bill

/expenses/cost-lines → Create bill

Tick cost lines — or tick nothing, which means the whole filtered set — and press Create bill, optionally grouping by tax rate and splitting one bill per order. Mixed currency, attribute, settlement party or owning branch cannot land in one bill — the selection splits into one bill per combination.

23

Get the bill approved

/approve/bill

This gate has teeth — for invoicing and write-off. Invoicing is frozen until the bill's latest review passes, and unlike the template gate at step 4 this one ships switched on. It does not protect the bill’s own total: a line can still be removed, and the re-total that follows is not frozen.

24

Issue the invoice

/expenses/bills → Invoicing

Pick invoice category and type, optionally type the real tax-authority number, and accept or edit the amount — pre-filled with the outstanding balance, so partial invoicing works. The bill flips to invoiced once fully covered.

Watch

No credit check happens here. Scope, gate, currency, branch, attribute and status are all checked; the counterparty's outstanding exposure never is.

25

Produce and hand over the document

/expenses/invoices

Preview the real document — seller (name, address, phone, contact — never a tax ID) and buyer (name, address, tax ID), fee lines with tax rate, two totals, payment terms derived from the company's days-payable — then save it as PDF or XLSX, which marks it downloaded. A void reverses the whole allocation atomically and makes the bill re-invoiceable.

Watch

The buyer block usually prints only a name — no address, tax ID or payment terms; see the break at step 20. There is also no send-by-email anywhere, although the quotation at step 9 has one, and no SST or GST: this is a commercial invoice, not a Malaysian tax invoice.

26

Record the money and settle it

/expenses/payments → /expenses/write-offs

Record the receipt — direction, settlement party, account, payment way, currency, amount, bank slip, date — then verify it against a bill, or, from the same dialog, a single billed cost line. There is no exchange-rate field. The amount spreads forward across that bill's cost lines in creation order. A wrong write-off is reversed, not edited.

Watch

Settlement attaches to the bill, never the invoice. With partial invoicing in use one bill legitimately carries several invoices — so “is this invoice paid?” has no answer on any screen, though both per-line splits exist to compute it.

27

Close the month

/expenses/cost-lines → Export table · /report/financial

Tick cost lines and export the customer statement, the debit note or the per-shipment profit sheet. Then work the five Financial Stat tabs: profit analysis, profit statement, receipts and payments, billing records, and ageing with days overdue (credit limit appears only in the ageing export).

Watch

The three month-end documents have no menu entry of their own — they open from an export dialog on two screens: the fee ledger (no ticks means the whole filtered set) and the order’s own expense page.

──
Chain break · The job never ends

Nothing in the system can mark a shipment shut out, finished, locked or archived. The server implements all three lifecycles with transition tables and guards; no screen calls any of them. Every job stays open forever, and the ledgers' own Finished filter returns nothing on every trade.

What to fix first

The seven nouns of the chain — quote, order, fee, bill, invoice, write-off, statement — each have a real table, a real router and a real screen. Most of what is missing is the arrows between them; the money stages also get their own arithmetic wrong. Ranked by consequence:

0 of 18 done

  1. 01 The customer’s identity does not survive the journey Carried onto the order, dropped at the cost line. The invoice still prints the buyer’s name but loses the address, tax ID and payment terms — and with no settlement company, the bill never gets a due date. Repairs
  2. 02 Every cross-currency settlement books a fabricated exchange gain or loss new The payment form has no exchange-rate field, so every payment is stored at a rate of 1 and cannot be corrected. The write-off derives its settled rate from that, so a payment settled against a bill in a different currency posts a gain or loss that never happened — unless the payment is already in the branch’s reporting currency. Repairs
  3. 03 Bills from the happy path can never become overdue new No settlement company reaches the bill, so the payment-terms trigger never fires and the due date stays empty. Ageing counts days overdue from the due date alone, so these bills show a blank Days overdue, sort last, and never trip the workbench’s Overdue chip — finance reads “nothing is late” on money that is. Repairs
  4. 04 Conversion turns every fee into a lump sum new Quantity and unit price exist on the quotation fee line and on the cost line, and the invoice and month-end statement both print them. The bridge carries neither, so those columns print blank on every job that started as a quote. Repairs
  5. 05 One shipment, two profits new The order’s gross-profit box and the per-shipment profit sheet use the fee row’s rate; Financial Stat uses the bill’s rate, dated the moment the bill was grouped. The same job reports two different margins depending on which screen you ask. Repairs
  6. 06 An approved bill’s total can still be rewritten new The bill flow ticks two of its six gates, and the two it skips protect the signed figure: a bill under review can have its fee set shrunk, and the re-total after removing a line carries no post-approval freeze, so an approved total can be rewritten downward. Booking a write-off needs a signature; reversing one needs none. Repairs
  7. 07 There is no organisation base currency new Currency is set per branch. Every total summed across branches adds MYR to VND and returns a number with no unit; the same-currency guard only protects a single bill. Repairs
  8. 08 Conversion still produces an unnumbered job Assign number now sits on the edit form conversion lands you on, so it is harder to miss — but conversion allocates nothing, and the lines it created keep the quotation number. One shipment’s invoice can print two different order numbers. Repairs
  9. 09 A shipment can never be closed Three guarded, audited lifecycle writers exist and no screen calls any of them — the bulk toolbar that did was deleted on 1 September 2026. Every job stays open, and nothing in the app can delete one either. Repairs
  10. 10 Paperwork cannot be filed against the job The link screen sends the wrong entity type and its picker is fed by the retired quote module. The per-order Documents card never renders at all, because server and client disagree on its shape. No K1/K2 declaration type exists. Repairs
  11. 11 The operational automation is an island The server already matches tracking jobs to orders by B/L number and attaches the customs stage, but leaves the 72-hour deadline out of that payload, and the card meant to show it never renders — so the countdown still cannot be seen from the order it belongs to. Repairs
  12. 12 To Receive is a dead end; Shipment review is an inbox nothing can post to To Receive has no link out. Shipment review’s rows do link to the bill, but no screen can submit a lading into it — and the company and contract review queues are empty by construction too. Repairs
  13. 13 Only email leaves an enquiry record WhatsApp and agent nominations have nowhere to land; walk-ins and phone calls can be typed straight into a company or a quotation, but leave no enquiry behind. Even an emailed enquiry has no outcome — a thread can only be closed, never marked won or lost. Repairs
  14. 14 Money leaves by hand at both ends No invoice email and no invoice permalink, and settlement applies to bills rather than invoices — so “is this invoice paid?” has no answer on any screen, though the per-line splits exist to compute it. Repairs
  15. 15 Nothing enforces the shape of a job Two auto-filled fields are all the server requires to create an order. Order status is a free-text box with no value set behind it, capped only at 200 characters. Repairs
  16. 16 Credit is captured and never enforced Credit limit is stored, shown on the company record, and printed only in the ageing report’s exported spreadsheet. No code computes exposure, and invoicing never consults it. Repairs
  17. 17 The invoice is not a Malaysian tax invoice No SST or GST anywhere, and the seller’s tax ID is hard-coded empty. The tax-authority number is typed in by hand against no allocator, so the real invoice is numbered somewhere else. Repairs
  18. 18 Haulage is a separate island A live truck map exists, with vehicle types and position history — but no driver, dispatch or leg entity, no proof of delivery, and no link from a truck to a job. Repairs

Method. Seven parallel readers over the shipped code — intake, quote, order, operations, documents, money, navigation — each report then adversarially verified against the cited files, and the surviving claims assembled into one ordered sequence.
Scope. Describes the system as built, not as intended. Steps are what a staff member does; the marked breaks are where the software stops helping.
Target chain. quote → order → fee entry → bill → invoice → write-off → month-end statement.
